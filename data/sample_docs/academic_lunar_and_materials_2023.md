# Lunar South Pole Landing Dynamics and Pragyan LIBS Spectroscopy (ISRO Peer-Reviewed Archive, 2023)

## Abstract
India launched the Chandrayaan-3 lunar exploration mission on July 14, 2023, aboard the LVM3-M4 launch vehicle from Satish Dhawan Space Centre. The Vikram lander successfully landed near the lunar south pole at 69.37 degrees South latitude on August 23, 2023. Following touchdown, the Pragyan rover deployed its Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS) payload and confirmed the unambiguous in-situ presence of sulfur (S), aluminum (Al), calcium (Ca), iron (Fe), chromium (Cr), and titanium (Ti) in the high-latitude regolith.

## Thermal and Power Subsystems
Surface temperature profiling via ChaSTE recorded a steep thermal gradient from 50 degrees Celsius at the surface to minus 10 degrees Celsius at a depth of 80 millimeters. The Vikram lander and Pragyan rover were solar-powered and designed for a single lunar day mission duration equivalent to 14 Earth days; neither vehicle carried radioisotope heater units (RHUs) for multi-year survival.

## Superconductivity Replication Audit (LK-99)
Independent crystallographic and magnetometry studies published in 2023 demonstrated that copper-substituted lead apatite (LK-99) is not an ambient-pressure room-temperature superconductor. The observed sharp resistivity drops near 378 Kelvin were caused by copper sulfide (Cu2S) phase-transition impurities rather than zero-resistance Meissner levitation.
